## Narrative Direction

Agentic commerce presents real business challenges now. Retailers can respond through expert competence strengthened by AI, supported by governed product information and connected systems. Begin with a bounded customer or operational task. A platform replacement, a new PIM, and guaranteed assistant placement are not prerequisites or promises.

**Evidence labels for review**

- **Published platform example:** a platform’s own product page, video, image, or announcement. It documents what that publisher says or shows; it is not an independent test or a Commonwork run.
- **Vendor-documented capability:** a vendor’s own documentation or product page describes a capability. Attribute it to the vendor; do not equate it with a performance result.
- **Vendor outcome claim:** a claim about results, growth, rankings, accuracy, or performance. Require stronger substantiation and identify the source and method.
- **Commonwork demonstration:** a capability actually run or demonstrated by Commonwork, with date, market, account/session context, permissions, and captured evidence. None is currently recorded.
- **Illustrative workflow:** a conceptual animation or scenario used to explain a process. Label it illustrative; it is not evidence of live capability.

### 5. Expertise First. AI Makes It More Effective.

- **Audience question answered:** How can expert marketing and operations work be strengthened by AI?
- **Proposed screen title / main message:** “Expertise first. AI makes it more effective.” This is Dirk’s recommended operating model, not a claim that all expert work is replaceable or that any workflow has already produced results.
- **Visual or demonstration:** Two compact work loops with judgement and approval points, not procedural checklists.
- **Short, untimed speaker notes:** “Experts define the task, decide what good looks like, and own the exceptions. AI can assist within that boundary. We should measure the work, not assume a result.”
- **Supporting sources:** [Begen’s AdMultify page](https://begen.ai/) supports only a vendor-described ad-creative variation capability. No source in the project documents either complete workflow as a Commonwork or retailer case.
- **Exact evidence boundary:** Both workflows are illustrative and proposed. No retailer ran them in this record; no result or measure has been observed. AdMultify is one possible marketing execution example, not the full marketing response.
- **Missing asset or decision:** Select a retailer-relevant task, human owner, baseline and success metric before making either workflow a real demo.

**Marketing workflow:** customer questions → expert judges suitability, differentiation, substantiated claims and representation → AI assists with research, summarisation or adapting approved material → expert approves facts/brand/legal/channel constraints; uncertain claims go to review → proposed measures: review time, correction/rejection rate and a campaign measure selected before testing.

**Operations workflow:** product/offer accuracy or source inconsistency → expert identifies the system of record, customer impact and safe correction → AI assists with comparing records, summarising a mismatch or drafting a work item → human approves data/system changes and handles exceptions → proposed measures: resolution time, recurrence, completeness and a preselected operational/customer measure.

### 6. Start with a Use Case. Build the Foundation to Scale It.

- **Audience question answered:** What product-data and systems foundation supports useful work now and scaling later?
- **Proposed screen title / main message:** “Start with a use case. Build the foundation to scale it.” Recommend clear product-information ownership, sufficient PIM capabilities and appropriate API/feed connections and controls.
- **Visual or demonstration:** A staged foundation: existing product/commerce systems → governed information and ownership → feeds/APIs with access, approval and exception controls. Mark PIM as a capability that may already exist in a platform, ERP or catalogue—not a prerequisite for every experiment.
- **Short, untimed speaker notes:** “First use what you have and identify the source of truth. Assess gaps. Invest where the task shows a repeated need. An API connection does not guarantee assistant access, discovery or placement.”
- **Supporting sources:** [Google Merchant Center product data specification](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112) and [Google Product structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product) for Google-specific requirements; Rierino [Commerce](https://rierino.com/solutions/commerce) and [platform documentation](https://docs.rierino.com/) for vendor-described platform capabilities.
- **Exact evidence boundary:** PIM/API is the speaker’s recommended foundation, not a universal prerequisite or platform requirement. Google rules apply only to relevant destinations. Rierino’s statements are vendor documentation, not independent results or a Commonwork implementation.
- **Missing asset or decision:** No retailer system inventory, data model, PIM configuration or API access has been reviewed. The diagram remains conceptual until a use case and system owner are selected.

**Readiness structure**

- **Start with existing systems:** identify product/offer owners, identifiers, attributes, claims, price/availability, delivery/returns, and existing exports or feeds.
- **Assess gaps:** field completeness, variant consistency, update latency, provenance, permissions, API/feed reliability, security and exception handling.
- **Invest where justified:** PIM capability, integration/orchestration, access controls, monitoring or data cleanup when the task exposes a repeated operational need.
