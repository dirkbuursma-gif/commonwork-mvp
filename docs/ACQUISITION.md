# Commonwork Acquisition Roadmap

Acquisition should follow a complete member value loop. Do not optimize for registrations before members can describe competence, discover relevant people and complete trusted introductions. Keep every invitation voluntary, contextual and separate from match ranking.

## Recommended sequence

| Release | Capability | Acquisition role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Batch 3 | Competence needs and transparent matching | Validate that a real business need produces relevant, explainable people matches. |
| Batch 4 | Double-opt-in introductions | Let matched members consent to a contextual introduction and confirm whether the conversation helped. |
| Batch 5 | Partner invitations, founding circles and connector pilot | Launch small partner cohorts, invite competence gaps without exposing private needs, and recruit trained connectors. |
| Batch 6 | Event-led onboarding and event-specific matching | Turn selected events into a guided first profile, need or introduction rather than a registration-only funnel. |
| Batch 7 | Commonwork Brief subscriptions | Offer an opt-in digest of relevant needs, competence and introduction updates with simple frequency controls. |
| Batch 8 | Working discussions and Ask the Network | Add structured group conversations after competence matching and introduction flows are producing useful activity. |

## Acquisition channels

### Partner invitation networks

Start with a small number of trusted professional communities whose members share a clear work context. Give each partner a reusable invitation link and a concise explanation of Commonwork's purpose. Attribute the referral source only with notice, and do not give partners access to member profiles, private needs or match results. Expand a partner cohort only after invited members complete profiles and reach useful conversations.

### Event-led onboarding

Use an event as a time-bounded reason to join, not as a substitute for a useful member workflow. Invite attendees to describe what they can contribute, what they want to learn and which conversations they welcome. Event-specific objectives and visibility require an explicit event scope; until that exists, use ordinary network needs and do not imply event membership changes matching access.

### Commonwork Brief subscriptions

Make Brief a deliberate opt-in subscription with clear topic, frequency and delivery controls. A Brief may surface relevant network needs, new competence areas and introduction follow-ups, but must not reveal private profiles, private needs, match identities or another member's activity. Every edition includes an easy unsubscribe and preference-management path.

### Competence-gap invitations

When an owner chooses to broaden a need that has few relevant people, offer an invitation they can review and send to their own trusted contacts. Do not expose the private need, identify a person as a match, or send invitations based on popularity, employer prestige or other proxy signals. The recipient decides whether to join, complete a profile and make the relevant competence discoverable; no invitation creates a match or introduction automatically.

### Founding circles

Recruit small, facilitated circles around a shared professional problem or sector. Give each circle a clear host, a limited term and a first useful action: complete a competence profile, post a real need or opt into a contextual introduction. Review member experience and safety at the end of each cycle before opening another circle.

### Connector programme

Invite trusted members to become Connectors after the double-opt-in introduction workflow is available. Provide consent and privacy training, a consistent way to explain why two people may benefit from meeting, and a clear decline/withdrawal path. Connectors never see private match scores, cannot override either person's consent and do not receive incentives tied to introduction volume.

## Activation stages

| Stage | Member action | Evidence of activation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invited | Accepts an invitation and signs in | Verified account and consent to join |
| Profile ready | Describes contribution and relevant competence | Completed, discoverable profile fields |
| Evidence ready | Adds an example and conversation preferences | At least one shareable example and explicit availability |
| Need ready | Describes a business outcome and essential competence | Need activated with an expiry and visibility choice |
| Match reviewed | Reads relevance reasons, evidence and unknowns | Saves, dismisses or gives relevance feedback |
| Introduction accepted | Both people consent to a contextual conversation | Double opt-in recorded privately |
| Conversation useful | Completes a conversation and follow-up | Member-reported usefulness, not message volume |

An invitation or registration alone is not activation. Members can pause or leave at every stage, and private preview identities never count as real activation.

## Acquisition metrics

- Invitation acceptance: invited people who verify an account divided by delivered invitations.
- Profile completion: real members who complete the competence profile divided by verified members.
- Shareable-evidence coverage: completed profiles with at least one shareable evidence item divided by completed profiles.
- Need activation: valid needs activated divided by need drafts started; report expiry and pause rates separately.
- Match review: active needs whose owner reviews at least one explained result divided by active needs with results.
- Match usefulness: owner feedback that a result is relevant, reported alongside dismissals and unknown/gap reasons.
- Double-opt-in rate: introductions accepted by both people divided by introduction requests sent.
- Useful conversation rate: accepted introductions later marked useful by at least one participant divided by completed introductions.
- Time to first useful conversation: elapsed time from verified signup to a member-reported useful conversation.
- Partner/circle quality: compare these activation and usefulness measures by consented referral source, not raw signups.

Do not optimize acquisition using follower counts, message volume, time spent, response pressure or match volume. Keep match feedback separate from acquisition attribution and never use demographic or prestige proxies to target invitations.

## Principles

- Partner invitations should lead to a useful competence or need workflow, not an empty directory.
- Events activate the member network; they do not replace matching and introductions.
- Referral attribution must not change match ranking or member visibility.
- Email digests require explicit opt-in and straightforward unsubscribe controls.
- Acquisition reporting should measure completed profiles, relevant matches, accepted introductions and useful conversations—not registrations, followers or time spent.
- Do not build broad social posting, mass messaging, payments or recruitment pipelines as acquisition shortcuts.
